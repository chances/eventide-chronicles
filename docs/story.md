# Story

This document defines the three-act story structure for The Eventide Chronicles. It is an internal planning document, not player-facing copy. Voice, tone, and terminology come from [branding.md](branding.md). Canon status comes from [lore.csv](lore.csv). The hidden premise remains only in [spoilers.md](spoilers.md).

## Story Shape

The story follows Toni Mueller through a grounded criminal world that gradually becomes difficult to trust. The first act establishes her daily reality and ends when that reality develops a visible crack. The second act turns the crack into a personal crisis. The third act forces the competing accounts of events into one final confrontation.

The structure uses an unreliable account, restrained dialogue, and deniable irregularities. Early events should support an ordinary explanation. Later events may challenge that explanation, but they should not explain the hidden premise before the final act.

## Act One: The Work She Knows

### Purpose

Act One establishes Toni, her immediate goals, the geography of her world, the cell phone, and the people who define her routine. It moves from ordinary survival to increasingly dangerous work. Toni does not begin with a desire to solve a mystery. She wants to get through the day and eventually go home.

### Story Missions

1. **Intro**: Toni enters the city and learns the basic rules of her immediate surroundings. The opening direction sends her to destroy or dump a car carrying unexplained evidence from an earlier incident. The player learns the practical rules for collecting objects and damaging vehicles, but neither Toni nor the player learns why the car matters.
2. **Cop**: A witness sees Toni destroying or dumping the car and calls the police. The caller gives Toni's description, and the responding officers recognize it from an older, incomplete file. Police pressure turns the city into an obstacle and forces Toni to learn how to move through it, while Toni has no idea why the police already seem to know who she is.
3. **Hotel**: Toni reaches the hotel and crosses a new boundary in the city. The hardware-store job introduces the mod's darkly practical criminal logic.
4. **Work**: Toni meets her boss and performs a delivery job. The mission establishes their antagonistic relationship, her precarious employment, and the ordinary routine beneath the violence.
5. **Cart**: Toni carries out an assassination in a golf cart. The job pushes her from difficult work into an act that cannot be treated as routine.

### Act Break: The Call After The Hit

After the assassination, Toni receives a call from her boss. He confirms the job and begins giving her another instruction, but the call drops before he finishes. Toni goes to the expected meeting point because she wants to complete the interaction and go home.

The boss's car is there, still running, but empty. There is no clear sign of a struggle. The phone rings again. The call appears to concern one of Toni's friends, but the voice reaching her through the phone is a strange man's voice. The call ends without an explanation and without a reliable account of what Toni has heard.

This is the first major crack in Toni's reality. It is not a conventional villain reveal, a surveillance plot, or a solved disappearance. The player should be able to dismiss each detail separately while feeling that the combination is wrong.

### The Police Trigger

The police encounter has a clear immediate cause, even though Toni cannot explain the history behind it. During `Intro`, the opening direction treats the car as evidence that must be destroyed or dumped. The player follows that direction as a practical objective and learns the pickup and vehicle-health systems. The car's contents and connection to Toni remain unexplained.

A witness sees Toni handling the car and calls the police. The caller describes her, and the police recognize the description from an older, incomplete file. The cop therefore arrives because Toni is already known to the police in a way she cannot account for. The game should make the call and description legible enough that the player understands the immediate cause without learning the older case.

The cop encounter should not establish a complete conspiracy or explain Toni's history. Its function is to make the city react to an act Toni performs without understanding, then leave the player with a concrete question: what happened before the game that put her in the file?

### Optional Side Activities

The first act may include at least three optional activities that support free-roam play without interrupting the story:

- **Armed Robbery Crime Spree**: A repeatable crime activity built around escalating wanted pressure.
- **Credit Card Theft**: A pedestrian-focused activity that introduces the credit-card economy and related uses.
- **Rooftop Rampage**: A contained combat challenge that provides a clear, replayable burst of action.

Side activities are optional. They should not be required to understand the story or reach the Act One cliffhanger.

## Act Two: The Missing Center

### Purpose

Act Two begins after **“The Call After The Hit.”** Toni tries to return to ordinary goals, but the disappearance of her boss and the unstable phone contact make ordinary explanations harder to maintain. The act shifts the story from Toni's job to Toni's interpretation of events.

The boss dies or is otherwise incapacitated during Act Two, not during the first-act cliffhanger. His fate should be experienced as a consequence that Toni cannot place, rather than as the reveal of a clear human conspiracy.

### Dramatic Movement

- Toni searches for a practical explanation and follows the remaining work and phone leads.
- Accounts of the boss, the assassination, and Toni's own actions begin to conflict.
- Phone calls and familiar places carry small inconsistencies that can still be explained away.
- The narrator remains unreliable, but each misleading account must have a discoverable reason for being misleading.
- Toni's emotional state becomes visible through changes in mood, coherence, and the stability of the world around her.
- The player sees enough evidence to question the ordinary reading of events, but not enough to state the hidden truth.

Act Two should end when Toni can no longer treat the contradictions as isolated mistakes. The conflict is no longer only what happened to her boss. It is whether Toni can trust the account through which she experiences the city.

## Act Three: What The Account Leaves Out

### Purpose

Act Three brings the story's competing explanations together. Toni follows the final chain of consequences from the boss's incapacitation, the phone calls, and the earlier work. The act should not simply provide a detective's solution. It should force a choice about which version of events can be believed, and what Toni has been refusing to see.

### Dramatic Movement

- Earlier irregularities return with clearer significance.
- The surface explanations fail in a pattern rather than as isolated incidents.
- The narrator's account is challenged by details the player has already encountered.
- Toni reaches the emotional and perceptual limit of the world she has been navigating.
- The final confrontation answers the central questions without erasing the ambiguity that gives the story its tone.
- The ending recontextualizes the first act, including the still-running car and the strange phone voice.

Answers arrive at the end of Act Three. Player-facing material before that point should preserve deniability and should not state or hint at the hidden premise. See [spoilers.md](spoilers.md) for the protected story truth.

## MVP Boundary

The MVP is the first act and its cliffhanger:

`Intro → Cop → Hotel → Work → Cart → The Call After The Hit`

The target is three to five story missions and less than two hours for a first-time player. The three optional side activities are supporting content, not prerequisites. The MVP succeeds when a new player can install the mod, understand Toni's immediate situation, experience a coherent opening escalation, use the cell phone as part of the story, and reach the unresolved first-act break without a major crash or confusing unfinished state.

The boss's Act Two death, the final answers, and the full three-act conclusion are outside the first playable story-slice milestone, but the MVP must plant the emotional and structural foundations they depend on.
